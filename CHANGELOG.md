# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Added signed usage receipts and reporter controls to `apps/05_usage_metered_saas` (#15). `recordUsage` trusted its caller completely, so one compromised reporter — a metering service, a gateway, a device fleet — could bill any tenant anything.
- A receipt splits what an attacker has to steal in two: the reporter principal that submits it and a registered P-256 device key that signs it where the usage was observed, possibly offline. Neither alone forges usage, each is revoked on its own, and `requireSignatures` closes the unsigned path to a stolen principal. Keys are registered only by controllers, since a reporter that could add its own would let a stolen principal mint one.
- The receipt binds the metering canister, reporter, key, tenant, units, category, idempotency key and observation time under a versioned domain. Binding the canister is what stops a staging receipt billing a production tenant; `receiptSpec()` publishes it with every other rule a signer follows.
- `mo:ecdsa` 8.0.1 accepts high-S signatures: it normalizes `s` when it constructs a `Signature`, so the low-S check inside its `verify` never fires. Found by a pinned high-S vector; the range and low-S checks are now made on the values as received, because accepting both halves of a signature pair would give every receipt a twin.
- A resubmitted receipt returns the original event and records nothing; a different receipt reusing an idempotency key is a conflict. Receipts may be at most 5 minutes ahead and 7 days old and never older than their key; a retired key keeps what it signed before rotation, and a compromised key is refused whatever time it claims, because a thief chooses `observedAt`.
- Reporter policies scope tenants and categories and cap units per event and per tumbling window — the ceiling on what a compromised reporter with a working key can inflate — on the signed and the unsigned path alike. `getReporter` reports health per window, rejections by reason, and an anomaly flag; `exportUsageAudit` lets an auditor re-verify every signed event with `node:crypto`.
- Measured on pocket-ic 14.0.0, verifying one receipt costs about 0.7 billion cycles (~1.8 billion instructions); a replay about 0.7 million. `maxBatch` is 16 so a full batch stays well inside the 40-billion-instruction message limit, and receipts are meant to carry aggregated usage, not one request each.
- App 05's replica suite goes from 48 to 121 checks, 586 across all five, covering the whole test plan (key rotation, offline batch, future timestamp, reporter compromise) and an upgrade. Candid and stable data are additive; the new dependency is `mo:ecdsa` 8.0.1 (Apache-2.0).
- Added AI tool and model attestations (#39). A manifest's `ai` block is the creator's own account, useful and weak; an `AIGenerationAttestation` is the same statement signed by the provider or local tool that produced the output, and an `AIUsageReviewCredential` is an organization's review of it. Both are Verifiable Credentials from #11, so proofs, issuer policy, key rotation, compromised keys and status-list revocation are the ones already tested there. `protocol/AI_ATTESTATION.md` has the schema, the levels and the threat model.
- The verifier reports one of four levels — `none`, `self-asserted`, `tool-signed`, `organization-reviewed` — always with the evidence behind it. What an issuer can raise the level to is fixed by its policy `kind`: a provider cannot review its own attestation into `organization-reviewed`, and an attestation from an issuer the policy does not know adds nothing beyond self-assertion.
- Attestations cannot be replayed. Each is bound to the SHA-256 of what the tool produced, which must be the registered artifact or a parent the manifest declares (the draft the creator then edited). Anything else is reported as a replay, and presenting one alongside a genuine attestation caps the level at `self-asserted`. A review names the attestation it read by digest, so it does not carry over to a reissue under the same id.
- Prompts are never needed publicly. The attestation and the manifest carry `SHA-256("icp-ai-prompt:v1" || 0x00 || salt || 0x00 || prompt)`, and the creator discloses salt and prompt only to an auditor. The suite shows why the salt matters: three guesses recover the example prompt from a plain SHA-256 and find nothing against the sealed commitment.
- A tool attesting AI use the manifest does not disclose is an understatement and an error, not an upgrade: the record binds the creator's disclosure, and a tool cannot fix it after the fact. `model.version` is required because an alias names a moving target; the alias is kept and the report shows what it resolved to. Control characters and bidi overrides in model and provider names are refused even in a correctly signed attestation — the signature makes the issuer accountable, not the text safe. 51 offline checks in `run_offline_checks.sh`.
- Added counterclaims and a dispute workflow to `apps/01_creator_proof_registry` (#8). Revocation was the only thing a record could say about itself, and only its owner could say it; a third party who believed a record was wrong — someone else made the work, it existed before the commitment, its AI disclosure is false — had nowhere to put that.
- The record is never touched. A dispute is a side structure keyed by record id, and no dispute endpoint can change a record, its status or its certified digest. The suite checks that an upheld counterclaim leaves the record digest byte-identical and still attested by the subnet.
- Technical status and outcome are separate. `#active` and `#revoked` say what the owner did; a determination says what a registered authority concluded under its own published policy. The registry records both, never marks a record false, and never picks between two authorities that disagree — `disputeSummary` counts the disagreement and the reference wording says so. A concession by the respondent is a statement, not an outcome.
- Due process: nobody determines an unanswered counterclaim inside the respondent's 14-day window, and silence does not block it after that; one determination per authority per round; each side appeals once, within 30 days; the claimant withdraws only before any determination.
- The respondent is the attributed creator's current root, resolved the way registration is, so the delegate who signed a record and a key rotated away cannot answer for the identity.
- Every transition is an event in a per-dispute hash chain whose head is certified under `["dispute", id]`, and a dispute is `Dispute.apply` folded over its log. `exportDispute` is a self-contained document whose single witness reveals both the record digest and the log head; `test/dispute-log.mjs` is the reader, written from the grammar in `docs/DISPUTES.md` as a second implementation. It rejects an edited event, a dropped event, a served state that disagrees with its log, an altered record, and an old log presented with the current certificate — the last one only at the certified head, which is the step a reader must not skip.
- Abuse controls without a payment rail: five filings per claimant per rolling day (withdrawing does not give one back), caps on unresolved disputes per claimant and per record, one unresolved dispute per claimant and record, and suspension after three `#abusive` findings in 90 days. `#dismissed` does not count; a weak claim is not a bad-faith one. All of this is per principal, so a Sybil campaign is bounded per record but not in total; a bond is the control without that weakness and needs #12. Refusals for volume return `#rateLimited { retryAt }` in a new `DisputeError` type rather than a new tag on the released `Error`.
- Private evidence is `#sealed { custodian }`: a digest and who holds it, with no field a URI could be left in, and a custodian containing `://` is refused.
- App 01's replica suite goes from 118 to 214 checks, 447 across all five with #9, covering the whole test plan: false report spam, private evidence pointer, appeal, conflicting authorities. `test/Dispute.test.mo` covers the rules in the interpreter and pins two events' bytes and hashes produced by the JavaScript reader. Candid is additive (14 methods); stable data gains new maps and a second certified-tree label.
- Added W3C Verifiable Credential issuing and verification (#11). A principal proves that someone holds a key, not that they are a member of an organization, may publish for a creator, or reviewed a record — those are claims someone else makes, and a VC is the standard container for them. Three types: `CreatorMembershipCredential`, `DelegatedAuthorityCredential`, `ProvenanceReviewCredential`, VC Data Model 2.0, subjects named by principal and nothing else.
- Proofs are Data Integrity `eddsa-jcs-2022`, because JCS is RFC 8785 and the repository already implements it to the byte: one canonical form across the protocol, and verification that needs no JSON-LD processor and no network. The W3C Recommendation's own vector is reproduced step by step down to the 64-byte signature, the one check that does not compare this implementation with itself.
- An unknown issuer is a warning, never a success: the verdict is `unknown-issuer`, the CLI exits 2, and the report says the signature is valid and why that is not enough. Authority is per credential type, and a credential naming a trusted issuer but signed by another key is `issuer-mismatch`.
- Issuer rotation is key history in the verifier's policy, as #7 does it on-chain: a did:key cannot rotate, since a new key is a new DID. A superseded key keeps validating what it signed before its retirement; a *compromised* key validates nothing, including what it claims to have signed earlier, because `created` is written by the signer and a thief can backdate it.
- Revocation and suspension use Bitstring Status List v1.0, checked like a credential: it must be signed by the credential's own issuer (or anyone could serve an all-zero list), carry at least 131,072 entries (the privacy crowd), match purpose and id, and decompress under a 16 MiB cap. An unavailable list fails closed; a policy may choose to warn instead, and the report says so.
- With a registry adapter, a credential may not claim more than the chain grants. A delegation credential is rejected when its on-chain delegation is revoked, expired, differently scoped, or ends before the credential does; a membership credential goes stale when the creator rotates its root. `tools/pocket-ic/vc.test.mjs` shows it on app 01 (20 checks, Replica workflow): the credential never changes, the chain does, and the verdict follows the chain.
- `protocol/VERIFIABLE_CREDENTIALS.md` includes a personal data minimization review and a selective disclosure note — SD-JWT (RFC 9901), `ecdsa-sd-2023`, `bbs-2023` — recommending none yet for credentials that carry nothing worth hiding. 80 offline checks in `run_offline_checks.sh`.
- Added a C2PA bridge for PNG content credentials (#10). A registry record is only useful to someone holding its URL, and media travels without URLs; C2PA is what editors, CMSs and platforms read. A credential now carries an `io.github.hjosugi.icp-proof` assertion naming the canister, the record and its hashes, and `protocol/tools/c2pa-bridge.mjs verify` checks the credential *and* the certified record before it says anything. Specification checked: C2PA 2.4 (April 2026).
- The credential and the record are checked against different roots — X.509 for the signer, the IC root key for the record — and reported separately before they are combined. A valid credential naming a revoked record is `revoked`, not `verified` and not `invalid`; a record that does not exist is a broken link (`unlinked`), and a registry that did not answer is `unverifiable`, because reporting an outage as a broken link would let the network slander a valid credential.
- The hard binding and the registry agree by construction. `c2pa.hash.data` covers the file minus the manifest store, the store is inserted as one `caBX` chunk and nothing else changes, so the bound bytes are the original file — whose SHA-256 is the record's `artifactHash`. A stripped copy still resolves to its record through `getByArtifactHash`; the replica test does exactly that.
- Credential laundering is closed from the record side. Anyone holding the asset can sign a credential naming the creator's record, and every hash in it will match, because it is the same asset. The creator's committed manifest can declare its C2PA signing keys (`extensions["io.github.hjosugi.c2pa"].signers`, SHA-256 of the SubjectPublicKeyInfo), and a credential from an undeclared key is `invalid`.
- The validator requires the data-hash exclusions to cover exactly the manifest store: an exclusion over `IDAT` would let every pixel change under a valid signature. The bridge also refuses to understate AI use — a record disclosing `generate` cannot be credentialed as a digital capture — and reads the ICP link only from a signed, *created* assertion, never a gathered one.
- CBOR, JUMBF, COSE_Sign1 and a hand-written DER issuer for the Ed25519 test PKI, all dependency-free, so the 116-check offline suite runs in `run_offline_checks.sh`. `tools/pocket-ic/c2pa-bridge.test.mjs` runs the path end to end on pocket-ic 14.0.0 in the Replica workflow (22 checks): online and offline verification with the BLS certificate checked against the subnet key, tampered bundles, revocation, and a revoked record spliced back to active failing certification.
- `protocol/tools/c2pa-crosscheck.mjs` checks both directions against c2patool 0.27.22, outside CI: the published example is `Trusted` with the test root as anchor and fails only `signingCredential.untrusted` without it, and a credential c2patool writes — thumbnails, ingredient and all — validates here. Two byte-level rules were settled by measuring its output rather than by reading: an assertion's hash covers the superbox payload, and the exclusion covers the whole chunk including length and CRC.
- `protocol/examples/c2pa/` is deterministic — fixed image, test keys derived from public strings, fixed ids and salts — and the suite asserts it is byte-identical to what the code produces. Its record's owner is the anonymous principal, which the registry refuses, so it cannot be mistaken for a real proof. `scripts/validate_kit.py` now schema-validates its manifest alongside the other two examples.
- Fixed the Merkle tree rules behind `apps/02_merkle_anchor` and made the canister verify proofs (#9). The anchor stored a root and a leaf count and nothing said how either related to a leaf, so a proof was checkable only by whoever had built the tree, with whatever rules they had used. `protocol/MERKLE_V1.md` specifies `icp-merkle:v1`: 32-byte leaf values hashed as `SHA-256(0x00 || "icp-merkle:v1" || leaf)`, nodes as `SHA-256(0x01 || left || right)`, and the RFC 9162 tree shape — an RFC 9162 tree whose entries are the domain-tagged leaves.
- Every rule answers a known attack: the `0x00`/`0x01` prefixes stop an interior node being presented as a leaf, the RFC 9162 split never duplicates an odd node so `[a, b, c]` and `[a, b, c, c]` do not share a root (CVE-2012-2459), pairs are ordered by position rather than sorted so a proof binds the leaf's index, and the path length is fixed by `(index, size)` and checked before anything is hashed. Each has a vector.
- An RFC 9162 audit path does not authenticate the tree size: leaf 2's path is the same in every tree of 5 to 8 leaves. That is harmless only when the verifier supplies the size, so `verifyProof` takes the anchored `leafCount` and has no size parameter, and `provenance-cli.mjs merkle-verify` refuses to run without a root and leaf count from the anchor. The test suite asserts the property rather than leaving it as prose.
- Added `verifyProof` (one leaf, RFC 9162 audit path), `verifyMultiproof` (up to 256 leaves, one canonical encoding: the subtrees with no proven leaf, in walk order, consumed exactly) and `merkleSpec` to the canister. A verdict returns `included` and the batch `status` side by side, so a leaf of a revoked batch is reported as both, and `computedRoot`, which `getByRoot` turns into the batch a stray proof actually belongs to.
- `anchor` now accepts only `treeVersion = "icp-merkle:v1"` with `hashAlgorithm = "sha256"`; the field had been free text and the replica suite was anchoring `rfc6962`. Batches anchored earlier keep what they declared and `verifyProof` refuses them with `#conflict` instead of reinterpreting their roots. The replica suite installs the `v2026.09.22` build, anchors such a batch, upgrades, and checks exactly that, which is why `replica.yml` now fetches tags.
- Two implementations sharing no code: `protocol/tools/merkle.mjs` (builder, prover, verifier, and `merkle-root` / `merkle-prove` / `merkle-verify` in the CLI) and `backend/src/Merkle.mo`. The JavaScript core passes all 98 transparency-dev/merkle inclusion probes and reproduces the RFC 6962 reference roots; `protocol/test-vectors/merkle/vectors.json` pins 15 trees (up to the anchor's 1,000,000-leaf maximum and its 20-hash path), 7 multiproofs and 21 rejections, each rejection pinned to its exact message or to `included: false`.
- The Motoko test data is generated, not transcribed: `protocol/tools/merkle-vectors.mjs` renders both vector files into `apps/02_merkle_anchor/test/MerkleVectors.mo`, and the offline checks fail if it is stale, so `test/Merkle.test.mo` always runs the canister's verifier over everything that was published.
- Added ledger-verified payment to `apps/03_license_marketplace` (#12). A buyer used to submit `(ledger, block, receiptHash)` and the seller decided whether to believe it, so a seller who trusted a forged receipt issued a real licence. A listing whose ledger a controller has registered now takes the other path: the buyer opens a payment intent, pays with an ordinary `icrc1_transfer`, and confirms with the block index, and the canister reads that block from the ledger itself with ICRC-3 `icrc3_get_blocks`. The buyer supplies a pointer; every property of the payment is read from the ledger.
- A block pays for an intent only if it is an ICRC-1 transfer (`1xfer`, or `tx.op = "xfer"` on older ledgers) to the seller — ICRC-1 subaccount-normalized — from the buyer, of at least the price, with the intent's memo, stamped inside the intent's window, and never used before. The fee is the sender's cost on top, so a buyer who counted it inside the price underpaid by exactly the fee, and is refused.
- The memo is `SHA-256("icp-license-intent:v1" || 0x00 || marketplace principal || 0x00 || intent id)`. Deduplication by `(ledger, block)` is only as wide as one canister, so without the principal one payment could be claimed on every marketplace that pays the same seller; and because intent ids are predictable, a payment made before its intent opened is refused by time, not by memo.
- `confirmPayment` is safe to repeat. A ledger that rejects or times out changes nothing and the same call succeeds later; a confirmation whose response was lost returns the same grant when repeated and issues nothing new. Everything that decides the outcome is re-read after the ledger call, because a concurrent confirmation or a sale that empties the supply can run while it waits.
- On a verified listing `submitPurchase` is refused — the manual flow is exactly how a forged receipt becomes a grant — and the `(ledger, block)` index is shared by both flows, so neither can re-spend a block the other used. `getGrantPayment` returns the ledger's own account of the payment behind a grant, or nothing for a grant a seller settled by hand. Token decimals, symbol and fee are read from the ledger at registration and stated on every intent.
- The adapter is `Icrc3.mo` and `Payment.mo`, and uses only `icrc1_symbol`, `icrc1_decimals`, `icrc1_fee` and `icrc3_get_blocks`, following archive callbacks. `test/fixtures/MockLedger.mo` is a local ICRC-1/ICRC-3 ledger the replica suite makes real transfers on. App 03's suite goes from 44 to 110 checks, covering the test plan — wrong recipient, underpayment, duplicate block, timeout after success — and every other way a receipt can lie that we could construct.
- Paying for a copy that sells out before confirmation records the intent as `#paidSoldOut` and consumes the block: the payment is real and owed back, and refunds are #13. Candid is additive; the new methods return a new `PaymentError` rather than adding tags to `Error`. Stable data gains side tables only.

## v2026.09.22

- Added creator identity, key rotation and scoped delegation to `apps/01_creator_proof_registry` (#7). A record's creator used to *be* the principal that signed it — one key, forever — so a lost key was a lost body of work, an organization could not offboard a member without abandoning what they registered, and nobody could say "this assistant may publish for me, on this project, until March".
- A record keeps the principal that signed it, permanently. Rewriting old records to point at a new key is the obvious implementation of rotation and it is falsifying provenance: at the moment of registration that key really was the signer. Rotation appends to a key history instead, and attribution is resolved by reading it.
- Attribution is a side index, not a field on `ProofRecord`. Records written before identity existed stay byte-identical, the certified digest from #6 is untouched, and no stable-data migration is needed.
- Identity is opt-in; being governed by one is not. A principal with no creator and no delegation registers exactly as before, but a rotated-away key, a revoked delegate, an expired delegate and an out-of-scope delegate are all refused — otherwise each could fall back to registering as itself and revocation and rotation would be decorative. The refusal does not say which applied, so a caller cannot probe for delegations it does not hold.
- Delegations are scoped to a collection or to the whole identity, carry a mandatory absolute expiry capped at 365 days, and are revocable. There is no "never expires": a delegation nobody has to renew is one nobody remembers to withdraw. Expiry is a deadline rather than a status change, so an expired delegation still reads `#active` and authorizes nothing — the suite asserts exactly that.
- A collection-scoped delegate registering outside any collection is out of scope, not in it. Reading `null` as "any collection" would leave the scope for the delegate to honour.
- Recovery is declared in advance by the root while it still holds the key, delayed by at least seven days, cancellable by either party, and visible from `getRecovery` the whole time. A recovery path that could be added afterwards would be a takeover path; the delay is what lets the current root notice one it did not ask for.
- App 01's replica suite goes from 66 to 118 checks, 280 across all five, covering every acceptance criterion and every case in the issue's test plan, including concurrent rotations and offboarding.
- Added certified queries to `apps/01_creator_proof_registry` (#6). `getRecord` is a query, and a query response is not signed, so a boundary node, a proxy or a compromised frontend could change any field of it undetected — the wrong trust model for a registry whose product is "this record says what the creator committed to". `getRecordCertified` returns the subnet certificate and a pruned hash tree alongside the record.
- The certified digest covers **every field**, status included. Covering only the identity would leave `storageUri` — where the artifact actually is — alterable without detection, and a revocation an intermediary could suppress by continuing to serve the record as active would be cosmetic.
- The reader has to check that the witness root *is* the certified data, and that is the step that is easy to leave out and fatal to leave out: a witness that reconstructs internally but is rooted elsewhere describes some other tree. `tools/pocket-ic/certificate.mjs` is the reference reader, and the suite includes a witness captured before a mutation, which fails at exactly that step.
- The record encoding exists twice on purpose — `backend/src/RecordDigest.mo` and `test/record-digest.mjs` — because a verifier that asked the canister for the digest would be verifying nothing. The replica suite compares them on every certified record; `test/RecordDigest.test.mo` pins the bytes in the interpreter.
- Measured: certification adds roughly 0.9M–1.2M instructions to `reveal` and `revokeRecord`, an order of magnitude more than the commitment check. `put` is not monotonic in the record count, because the tree is a radix trie over the id bytes and insertion depth is set by where the new key diverges, not by how many keys exist.
- `scripts/check_candid_compat.py` and `tools/pocket-ic/harness.mjs` now ask `mops sources` for the package path instead of deriving it from `.mops/` directory names. `ic-certification` brings its own major versions of `core` and `sha2`, which mops resolves to `core@1` and `sha2@0`; a directory scan would have emitted `--package core` twice and let the compiler pick.
- Added replica integration tests for `apps/01`-`05` (#2): a shared harness in `tools/pocket-ic/` and one suite per application, 213 assertions on `pocket-ic` 14.0.0. `mops test` runs pure modules in the interpreter, which has no caller, no upgrade, and no clock — so most of what each application's README and `docs/UPGRADE_PLAN.md` claim was unproven until now.
- Each suite asserts the **variant** a rejection came back with, not merely that one did. A test that only checks "it failed" passes when the endpoint is broken for an unrelated reason.
- Every application is upgraded mid-suite and its counters, duplicate-suppression indexes, and authorization state are checked to survive — as is that ids keep counting up, since reuse would let a new record take a retired id.
- `apps/04` and `apps/05` move the replica clock to reach the bounty deadline and the quota rollover, paths the interpreter cannot express at all. `apps/05`'s admin gate is `Principal.isController`, which only a replica can answer.
- Found while building it: upgrading a `persistent actor` needs the `wasm_memory_persistence` install option, and `@dfinity/pic@0.22.0`'s `upgradeCanister()` has no field for it, so the harness calls `install_code` on the management canister directly. It passes `keep`; `replace` discards the heap, which would make every "state survives" assertion pass for the wrong reason.
- Added `.github/workflows/replica.yml` and `make replica-tests`.

The previous inventory had been produced in a working tree that also held gitignored agent skill directories and npm lockfiles, so it listed 375 entries of which 33 are in neither the repository nor the kit; the packaged artifact now carries 343.

## v2026.08.07

- The documentation site now rewrites links that leave it. A Markdown link to something that is not a staged page — a `.mo` source, a script, a directory — has nowhere to resolve inside the site, so it is redirected to the same path on GitHub. Without this, documentation could not link to the code it documents: the link would resolve in the repository, fail in the site, and take `mkdocs build --strict` down with it. Links to the root `README.md` are redirected to the staged `index.md` rather than off-site.
- Added `--self-test` to `scripts/build_docs_site.py`, seventeen pinned cases for the rewriter, run in CI before anything is staged. It covers a gap the other two gates cannot: `--strict` asks whether a link resolves, never whether it was supposed to be rewritten, so a rewriter that quietly stops rewriting still builds green and produces a site full of dead links. Disabling the rewriter fails nine of the seventeen.
- Documented the three documentation gates in `docs/06_TESTING_CI_DEPLOYMENT.md`, alongside the Candid ones.
- Brought `VALIDATION_STATUS.md` back in step with what has actually been run. It still listed `icp network start -d`, `icp deploy`, and the upgrade rehearsal as outstanding after they had been performed and recorded in `apps/06_distributed_llm/docs/MEASUREMENTS.md`, and said nothing about the documentation site. Understating what was verified is the safe direction to be wrong in, but it is still wrong.
- Dropped `apps/06_distributed_llm/tools/package-lock.json` from `FILE_INDEX.md` and `MANIFEST.sha256`. The app's own `.gitignore` excludes it, so it is in neither the repository nor the distributed kit; it was recorded because the inventory had been regenerated in a working tree where npm had run. Regenerating on a clean checkout is what removed it.
- Froze the v1 commitment layout and published its conformance vectors (#5). `protocol/COMMITMENT_V1.md` gives the byte-level ABNF, the principal rules, the error behaviour and the version negotiation rules; `protocol/test-vectors/commitment/vectors.json` gives 17 accept and 22 reject vectors, each accept vector pinning the full preimage in hexadecimal, its length and the commitment.
- The layout itself did not change. Principal validation did, and it needed to: it was a length check accepting any string of 5 to 100 characters, so `hello` and `not-a-principal` produced perfectly good commitments that no canister could ever match — the canister derives the same field from `Principal.toText(caller)` and never from a request. `protocol/tools/principal.mjs` now implements the textual form: base32 alphabet, CRC32 checksum, and a re-encode round trip that rejects a mis-grouped or wrongly padded spelling of the same blob. Two spellings of one creator would be two commitments for one creator.
- Uppercase principals are now rejected rather than silently lowercased. Accepting them would teach callers that the field is case-insensitive when the bytes that get hashed are not. Hexadecimal input stays case-insensitive, because it denotes octets, and there are accept vectors asserting both forms produce the same commitment.
- The real bounds replaced the guessed ones, in the protocol and in Motoko: a principal blob is 0 to 29 bytes, so the text is 8 to 63 characters, not 5 to 100.
- "No concatenation ambiguity" is now a checked property rather than a claim. `parsePreimage` recovers all three fields from the preimage bytes, and the suite asserts the round trip on every accept vector and that no two distinct triples share a commitment — which is what `salt-all-zero`, `salt-leading-zero` and `digest-all-zero` are for.
- Two implementations sharing no code with the reference reproduce every vector, both written from the specification: `crosscheck/commitment.rs` validating principals with `candid::Principal`, and `crosscheck/commitment.ts` with `@dfinity/principal`. Same verdict and same bytes on all 39. If the principal rules here disagreed with DFINITY's, or the document were not implementable from its own text, that is where it would show.
- `commitmentSpec()` gained `version`, `minPrincipalTextSize` and `maxPrincipalTextSize`, so the published rules are complete enough for a verifier to rebuild the preimage from the deployed canister rather than from a document it hopes matches. Both additions pass the Candid drift and subtyping gates; stable data is unchanged.
- The two vectors published before the freeze reproduce their previous commitments exactly. Freezing the layout did not move it.

- The provenance CLI now implements RFC 8785 (#4), replacing the recursive key sort it described as "a deterministic educational subset". The manifest digest is what the on-chain commitment binds, so two verifiers that disagree about the canonical bytes disagree about whether a record is valid.
- What was missing was never the serialization. RFC 8785 defines number formatting, string escaping and member ordering by pointing at ECMAScript, and `JSON.stringify` already got all three right. What was missing is everything `JSON.parse` discards first, so `protocol/tools/jcs.mjs` scans the text: a duplicate member name is silently resolved to the last occurrence, so `{"a":1,"a":2}` and `{"a":2}` hashed the same; a lone surrogate survives parsing here and is rejected outright by implementations built on UTF-8; `1E400` parses to `Infinity`, which RFC 8785 cannot serialize.
- Two deliberate deviations, both refusals, so the bytes produced for any accepted input still match every conformant implementation. Duplicate member names are rejected rather than resolved. An integer literal that does not round-trip exactly is rejected — `serde_jcs` rounds `12345678901234567890` to `12345678901234567000` the same way this would, and a manifest carrying file sizes and identifiers should not silently sign a different number.
- The round-trip test is exact, not digit-count based. An earlier version rejected any integer literal above `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`, which rejected `100000000000000000000` — the canonical form of `1e20`, and therefore its own output. The test suite now asserts idempotency on every vector, because a canonicalization with no fixed point is one two verifiers can disagree about by applying it a different number of times.
- Vendored the six official vectors from `cyberphone/json-canonicalization` and added 21 accepted and 26 rejected edge vectors, each pinned to its exact error message so failure behaviour is part of the contract. 87 assertions, offline, in `run_offline_checks.sh`.
- Added `protocol/tools/crosscheck.mjs`, which builds a throwaway `serde_jcs` project and installs the `canonicalize` npm package and runs all three implementations over the same inputs: 29 inputs canonicalized identically, 21 of the 26 rejections shared. It needs cargo, npm and network access, so it is outside CI by design, and it fails if the deviation list changes without the vectors being updated to match.
- Stated Motoko's responsibility: none. The canister takes a 32-byte digest as an opaque value and never parses JSON. A canister-side parser would cost cycles to re-derive a digest the caller already computed, and would be a second implementation to keep in step with the first.
- Manifests may now carry `"canonicalization": "RFC8785"`; absent means RFC8785, and a future scheme must name itself rather than change what an absent field means. The two published examples are unchanged — the old implementation produced byte-identical output for both, which is why the subset survived as long as it did.

- `apps/01_creator_proof_registry` now verifies the commitment on-chain (#3). `reveal` recomputes `SHA-256(domain || 0x00 || principalText || 0x00 || manifestHash || 0x00 || salt)` with `mo:sha2` and rejects a mismatch. Before this the canister stored the commitment and the revealed values side by side without ever comparing them, so a caller could commit to one digest and reveal something unrelated and the registry recorded it as a valid proof.
- The principal in the preimage is always the caller's, never a value from the request, so a commitment cannot be revealed under someone else's name.
- Added `RevealInput.algorithm`, an optional tag naming both the hash function and the layout. `null` means v1, so existing clients keep working. Nothing stores it: the algorithm picks the preimage and the digest is what `commit` already saved, so a wrong algorithm fails the same comparison as a wrong salt — no stable-state change and no migration.
- Added `commitmentSpec()`, so a verifier reads the layout and salt bounds off the canister instead of hardcoding them.
- Added `test/Commitment.test.mo`: the FIPS 180-4 SHA-256 examples, the preimage compared byte for byte, both published protocol vectors, both salt-size boundaries, and each bound field changed on its own.
- Measured the check at 97k–112k instructions across the accepted salt range (`bench/commitment.bench.mo`, `mops bench --replica pocket-ic`). Measuring it from outside the canister does not resolve it — an update call costs ~9.2M cycles whichever branch it takes.
- The mismatch is reported as `#invalidInput`, not a new `Error` tag. A new tag in a result is a break: Candid's special `opt` rule would let a released client decode it as `null` and silently see nothing, which is exactly what `scripts/check_candid_compat.py` rejects.
- `scripts/check_candid_compat.py` now passes every vendored package to `moc --idl`, not only `mo:core`. An app that imports anything else failed to compile, and a compile failure there reads as "no interface to compare", which would let real drift through.
- `scripts/vendor_core_offline.sh` now vendors non-`core` dependencies from the Mops global cache. `research-ag/sha2` stops tagging at `0.2.4`, so there is no pinned tree on raw.githubusercontent.com for the `0.2.5` the app uses.
